## Initial thesis

Oracle has a credible growth catalyst in cloud infrastructure and applications: FY2026 cloud revenue grew materially from the prior year. That growth supports further research into whether Oracle can earn attractive returns from its cloud investment cycle.

The initial recommendation is **watch / defer**, rather than initiate. The key reason is that the investor must determine whether cloud growth will translate into durable margins and free cash flow after the capital required to support it, while Oracle manages a large debt balance. The baseline therefore treats the cloud-growth opportunity as real but not yet sufficient, on its own, to support an investment decision.

## Known evidence and sources

- Oracle reported FY2026 cloud revenue of $33,989 million and total revenue of $67,357 million in its Consolidated Statements of Operations.
- Oracle reported cash and cash equivalents of $31,289 million as of May 31, 2026 in its Consolidated Balance Sheets.
- Oracle reported combined short- and long-term debt of $129,541 million as of May 31, 2026; retain the debt note as supporting evidence for this definition.
- Oracle reported FY2026 diluted weighted-average shares outstanding of 2,914 million in its earnings-per-share disclosure.
- Primary source for all facts above: Oracle Corporation, FY2026 Form 10-K, filed June 22, 2026, SEC accession 0001193125-26-277521.

## Consequential assumptions

1. Cloud revenue growth is a useful leading indicator of Oracle's long-run value creation, but it must ultimately produce sustainable cash flow and returns on invested capital.
2. The combined debt amount is the appropriate debt measure for an enterprise-value-to-equity-value bridge; cash and cash equivalents are treated as an offset to debt.
3. The FY2026 diluted weighted-average share count is a reasonable starting convention for per-share valuation, subject to later dilution analysis.
4. Oracle's future cloud demand, pricing, and customer retention will not deteriorate materially before the full valuation is completed.

## Unknowns to resolve

- How much incremental capital expenditure and financing will be needed to sustain Oracle Cloud Infrastructure growth?
- What portion of cloud growth is recurring, profitable, and defensible versus dependent on a limited number of large customers or contracts?
- How quickly can Oracle reduce leverage or refinance maturities without impairing investment in cloud capacity?
- What valuation range results under conservative, base, and downside revenue-growth and margin assumptions?
- How does Oracle's cloud growth, profitability, and leverage compare with relevant peers?

## Research plan

1. Build a three-statement historical model from Oracle's 10-K and subsequent filings.
2. Separate cloud, software, hardware, and services revenue; estimate segment growth, margins, and cash-flow implications.
3. Review the debt note, maturity schedule, interest expense, and refinancing needs; calculate net debt and leverage measures.
4. Forecast revenue, operating margin, taxes, capital expenditures, working capital, and free cash flow under base, upside, and downside cases.
5. Value Oracle with a DCF and relevant comparable-company multiples, then reconcile enterprise value to equity value using debt, cash, and diluted shares.
6. Test the thesis against peer outcomes and evidence from subsequent quarterly filings.
