# Oracle Corporation (NYSE: ORCL): cloud growth versus cash-flow and leverage

**Report date:** September 3, 2026  
**Decision:** Watch / defer a new position

## Investment view

Oracle is no longer best understood only as a mature enterprise-software company. Cloud revenue rose from $19.8 billion in FY2024 to $24.5 billion in FY2025 and $34.0 billion in FY2026, while total revenue rose from $53.0 billion to $57.4 billion and then $67.4 billion. The FY2026 acceleration came chiefly from cloud infrastructure: OCI revenue was $18.1 billion, up 77% year over year. [FY2024 10-K](sources.md#sec-filings) [FY2025 10-K](sources.md#sec-filings) [FY2026 10-K](sources.md#sec-filings)

That growth is real, and Oracle finished FY2026 with $638 billion of remaining performance obligations (RPO), including large AI contracts. However, RPO is not the same as recognized revenue or cash flow; Oracle says only 12% of RPO is expected to be recognized in the next 12 months and 34% in months 13–36. [Q4 FY2026 earnings call](sources.md#earnings-call)

The investment question is therefore not whether demand exists, but whether Oracle can turn contracted demand into durable free cash flow after funding data centers and servicing its debt. The evidence supports continuing research, not yet initiating a position.

## What changed in the business

Oracle sells cloud applications and cloud infrastructure, software licenses and support, hardware, and services. Its strategy is to offer cloud, on-premise, hybrid, and multicloud deployment choices. [FY2026 10-K](sources.md#sec-filings)

Cloud has become the largest revenue category. In FY2026, cloud represented 51% of revenue, up from 43% in FY2025; software revenue was essentially flat over the year. This mix shift makes Oracle more exposed to the economics of operating infrastructure rather than merely licensing software. [FY2026 10-K](sources.md#sec-filings)

The trend is mixed in an important way. Operating cash flow rose sharply in FY2026, but capex grew faster as Oracle expanded data-center capacity. Management expects capex to continue rising in FY2027 and afterward. [FY2026 10-K](sources.md#sec-filings)

## Balance-sheet and financing assessment

At May 31, 2026, Oracle reported $31.3 billion of cash and cash equivalents and $129.5 billion of combined current and long-term debt, or roughly $98.3 billion of net debt using cash as the offset. In FY2026, the company issued $42.7 billion of senior notes and $5.0 billion of mandatory convertible preferred stock, while scheduled debt repayments were $5.0 billion. [FY2026 10-K](sources.md#sec-filings)

For comparison, at May 31, 2025 Oracle had about $109.2 billion of total debt, $10.8 billion of cash, $20.8 billion of operating cash flow, and $21.0 billion of total equity. That implies approximately 4.7x net debt to operating cash flow and 5.2x debt to book equity. The debt-to-equity ratio is informative but imperfect because persistent share repurchases have reduced book equity; net debt relative to cash flow is the more useful risk signal here. [FY2025 10-K](sources.md#sec-filings)

FY2025 interest coverage was 4.94x: $17.678 billion of operating income divided by $3.578 billion of interest expense. This was adequate rather than distressed, but it was calculated before FY2026's additional borrowing and should be monitored as the cloud build-out continues. [FY2025 10-K](sources.md#sec-filings)

Management's June 2026 earnings materials said that Oracle raised $43 billion of debt financing and $5 billion of equity financing in FY2026, expected to raise about $40 billion more through debt and equity in FY2027, and did not expect additional debt issuance in calendar 2026. These are forward-looking management statements, not completed outcomes. [FY2026 earnings release](sources.md#recent-news-and-company-update)

## Demand visibility and management perspective

On the June 10, 2026 earnings call, management reported FY2026 cloud-infrastructure growth of 77%, RPO of $638 billion, and a net cash capex outlay of about $48 billion after prepayments and timing effects. It also stated that 12% of RPO was expected in the next 12 months and 34% in months 13–36. These figures provide meaningful demand visibility, but the long conversion period means delivery, customer-performance, capacity, and margin risks remain central to valuation. [Q4 FY2026 earnings call](sources.md#earnings-call)

Oracle's own 10-K identifies capacity planning, affordable data-center capacity, customer renewal and payment risk, and the economic return on large infrastructure investments as material issues. The filing says no individual customer represented 10% or more of FY2026 revenue, but certain OCI offerings are concentrated among a number of large customers. [FY2026 10-K](sources.md#sec-filings)

Independent news coverage after the June results focused on the tension between strong AI-cloud demand and Oracle's elevated capital spending and planned financing. That market reaction does not prove the thesis wrong, but it reinforces that free-cash-flow conversion—not headline RPO growth—is the issue investors are debating. [Reuters coverage, republished by MarketScreener](sources.md#recent-news-and-company-update)

## Risks and falsification test

The upside case is that Oracle converts RPO into high-utilization OCI revenue, lifts cash flow as capacity ramps, and reduces net leverage without curbing investment. The downside case is that capex and financing needs stay ahead of operating cash generation, or that a small number of large cloud customers delay, reduce, or fail to perform on contracts. [FY2026 10-K](sources.md#sec-filings)

**Falsification question:** What evidence would show that Oracle's cloud-revenue growth is not becoming durable, profitable free cash flow?

Evidence that would weaken the thesis includes: continuing negative free cash flow after a reasonable capacity-ramp period; materially higher net debt without a corresponding rise in operating cash flow; declining cloud margins or utilization; meaningful customer non-performance; or RPO that does not convert to recognized revenue on the disclosed time frame. This is an analytical inference based on Oracle's reported cash-flow, financing, and risk disclosures. [FY2026 10-K](sources.md#sec-filings) [Q4 FY2026 earnings call](sources.md#earnings-call)

## Current call

**Watch / defer (wait for more information).** Oracle has compelling OCI growth and a large contract backlog, but current evidence has not yet shown that the capital-intensive expansion will produce sustainable positive free cash flow while net debt stays manageable.

I would change this call to **initiate** after Oracle demonstrates several quarters of strong cloud-growth conversion into improving free cash flow and stable or declining net leverage, without relying on additional debt beyond its announced financing plan.
